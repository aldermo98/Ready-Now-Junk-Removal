## 9 ways to pay less for junk removal

### 1. Use free city pickup for what can wait

City of Sacramento residents get up to two free household junk pickups a year, West Sacramento residents two, and unincorporated Sacramento County one. Use those for the easy stuff. See our [free bulky item pickup guide](/blog/free-bulky-item-pickup-sacramento/).

### 2. Do it all in one trip

Most companies have a minimum charge. Two half-loads booked separately can cost more than one full load. Walk through the garage, attic, closets and yard before you call.

### 3. Stage items where they're easy to reach

If you can do it safely, move items to the garage, driveway or a ground-floor room. Less carrying means less labor. Most prices, including ours, assume first-floor or curbside access, while stairs and long carries can add a fee.

### 4. Sell or donate the good stuff first

Newer furniture, appliances and tools can sell on Facebook Marketplace or go to Habitat for Humanity's ReStore. Every item you remove first is space you don't pay for.

### 5. Team up with your neighbors

Junk removal companies save time and fuel when jobs are close together. At Ready Now we have a **Bundle 'N Save** program: when you and your neighbors book together, everyone pays less. Ask your street or post on Nextdoor.

### 6. Get an upfront quote from photos

Text clear photos of everything that's going. A good company will give you a price range up front and confirm the final price on site **before** loading. Be wary of anyone who won't quote until the truck is full.

### 7. Ask how they price

Ask: *"Do you charge by volume or weight? Are dump fees included? Are there extra fees for stairs or mattresses?"* A straight answer is a good sign.

### 8. Be flexible on timing

Weekday and midday slots are often easier to fill than Saturday mornings. If you can be flexible, ask whether there's a better time or rate.

### 9. Avoid unlicensed cash haulers

The cheapest quote from a flyer or random ad can cost you the most. Sacramento County warns that if items you paid someone to haul are **dumped illegally and traced back to you, you can be held financially responsible** for the cleanup. Hire a licensed, reviewed company.

## Cheap vs. fair: what to look for

A low price only helps if the job actually gets done right. Look for:

- **Real reviews** on Google (not just a website)
- **Upfront, volume-based pricing** with no surprise fees
- **Donation and recycling,** which keeps items out of the landfill and often lowers dump costs
- **Fast scheduling** when you need it
