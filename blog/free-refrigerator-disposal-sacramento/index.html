Quick answer: Most Sacramento-area residents can get rid of an old refrigerator for free. City of Sacramento customers get two free appliance and e-waste pickups a year (call 3-1-1), West Sacramento residents can use their two free Waste Management bulky pickups, and Sacramento County residents can drop off up to two appliances a day free at the North Area Recovery Station or Kiefer Landfill. If the fridge is heavy, upstairs or needs to go today, a junk removal company like Ready Now can carry it out and haul it to an appliance recycler for you.

Below is how each option works, what the catches are, and how to prep the fridge so your pickup isn't refused.

Why can't I just put a fridge out with the trash?

Refrigerators and freezers hold refrigerant, compressor oil and other materials that have to be removed before the metal can be recycled. That is why they need a scheduled appliance pickup or a facility that handles them. Some scrap yards won't take them at all. In West Sacramento, for example, J&M Metal Recycling does not accept any appliance that still contains freon or other liquids.

Leaving a fridge on the curb or in an alley without an appointment counts as illegal dumping, and it is a safety hazard for kids if the door is still on.

Free option 1. City of Sacramento appliance pickup

If you have City of Sacramento garbage service, this is the easiest free option.

  • Two free appointments per calendar year for appliances and electronics. These are separate from the city's household junk pickups.
  • Up to two appliances per appointment. Refrigerators, freezers, stoves, washers, dryers, dishwashers, water heaters, air conditioners and more are accepted. Commercial appliances are not.
  • Book it online through 311, with the Sac311 app, or by calling 3-1-1 ((916) 264-5011 from outside city limits).
  • The fridge must be empty, with no food or food residue.
  • Set it out in the street where the crew can reach it, no earlier than 24 hours before your appointment and by 6 a.m. that day. Early set-outs can be fined.
  • Cancel by 5 p.m. two business days ahead. A late cancellation or no-show uses up one of your free pickups.

Book the fridge as its own appointment, separate from any household junk pickup, since the two may be collected on different days.

Free option 2. West Sacramento bulky waste pickup

West Sacramento residents get two free bulky waste appointments per year (July 1 to June 30) through Waste Management. The city's recycling guide lists refrigerators and freezers under bulky waste pickup, and WM's residential brochure includes small and large appliances as eligible items.

  • Book it by calling Waste Management at (866) 844-1508.
  • Used both pickups? Call the city's Environmental Services at (916) 617-4590 to ask about a landfill voucher. Some restrictions apply.
  • Drop-off option: the Yolo County Central Landfill (44090 County Road 28H, Woodland) also accepts refrigerators, but it charges a per-item appliance fee. Check the county's current fee schedule before you go, and empty the fridge first.

Free option 3. Sacramento County drop-off (two a day)

If you have a truck and a helper, Sacramento County lets residents drop off up to two appliances per day for free, including refrigerators, at:

  • North Area Recovery Station, 4450 Roseville Road, North Highlands. Open 6:30 a.m. to 6 p.m. weekdays and 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. weekends. Arrive at least 15 minutes before closing. Phone (916) 875-5555.
  • Kiefer Landfill

Two rules matter here. The load must contain only free items (don't mix in regular trash), and you should remove fluids and any removable batteries first.

Residents of unincorporated Sacramento County (Carmichael, Fair Oaks, Rio Linda, Antelope and so on) also get one free curbside bulky pickup per calendar year through 3-1-1 or the SacGreenTeam app, with extra pickups at $25 each.

Free option 4. Give it away or donate it

A working fridge is worth something to someone. Post it free on Facebook Marketplace, Nextdoor or a local Buy Nothing group and the person who wants it will usually bring their own truck. Sacramento County also points residents to used household goods stores, churches, the Salvation Army and Habitat for Humanity of Greater Sacramento for working appliances. Call ahead, since most charities only take appliances that are clean, working and fairly new.

Free option 5. Ask about haul-away when you buy a new one

If you're replacing the fridge, ask the store whether removing the old one is included with delivery, and what it costs if not. Get the answer before you check out, since policies vary by retailer and by promotion.

How do I prepare a fridge for pickup?

  • Empty it completely and wipe out spills. The City of Sacramento won't take a fridge with food residue.
  • Unplug it a day ahead so the freezer can defrost, and keep towels under it.
  • Shut off and disconnect the water line if it has an ice maker. Leave that to a plumber if you're not comfortable doing it.
  • Tape the doors shut for the move, and never leave a fridge outside with the doors on and unattended.
  • Measure doorways. Many full-size fridges need the doors taken off to fit through.

When is it worth paying for fridge removal?

Free options work well if you have time, a truck and two strong people. A full-size refrigerator is heavy, tall and awkward to move, so paying makes sense when:

  • The fridge is in a garage, kitchen or upstairs and you can't safely get it to the curb
  • You need it gone today or this week, not whenever the next appointment opens
  • You've already used your free pickups for the year
  • You're clearing out more than one item, like a washer, dryer and old furniture too
  • You're a landlord or seller and the unit has to be empty for a showing or move-in
